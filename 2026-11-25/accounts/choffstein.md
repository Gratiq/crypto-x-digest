"Should we stack on top of equities or bonds?"

With return stacking and portable alpha, how you free up your beta creates a hurdle rate that your stack / alpha has to overcome.

I get asked about this one a lot, so I wanted to spend some time developing a more precise answer.  From Oct 31, 2021 to Oct 31, 2026:

- An equal-weight 2-, 5-, 10- and long bond futures ladder cost SOFR+17-29bp

- S&P 500 futures cost SOFR+54-59bp

- It can be quite variable

This might point to Treasuries being a better place to stack (despite everyone seemingly wanting to stack on equities...)

But we have to consider the details, like the fact that Treasury futures quietly make you an options seller and will have a higher tracking error to a US Treasury index.

## 2026-11-24 19:09 UTC · пост

As end-of-year distributions approach, there can be confusion around why the distributions that managed futures or commodity funds pay might not immediately correlate to the returns generated by commodities in the current tax year.

The reason is timing. 

Most of these funds hold their futures through a wholly owned offshore subsidiary. The fund doesn't pick up the subsidiary's gains as they happen. 

Instead, the subsidiary's net income for its full fiscal year comes up to the fund in one lump at the subsidiary's year-end, and it arrives as ordinary income.

Funds then have to distribute that income by calendar year-end to avoid a 4% excise tax. 

So, if a fund's fiscal year ends January 31, this December's distribution reflects gains earned through last January. 

Anything earned since February won't show up until next year's distribution. And if the subsidiary has a net loss for its year, that loss stays trapped in the subsidiary and doesn't offset the fund's other income.

To see where a fund sits on this timeline, look up its Form N-CSR (its annual report) on SEC EDGAR. The fund's fiscal year-end is printed on the cover page. 

For example, $KMLM's fiscal year ends March 31, while $PDBC's ends October 31. 

The bigger the gap between the year-end and December, the less a fund's distribution reflects what  (trading) commodities did this year.
